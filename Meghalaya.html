Gastronomy & Culture

Taste of Meghalaya: Indigenous Khasi Flavours

🍲

Savor authentic red hill rice delicacies, roasted sesame curries, and fermented soybeans:

🍚 Jadoh (Red Rice Pilaf) Khasi Staple

Aromatic indigenous red hill rice simmered gently with rich pork or chicken broth, ginger, garlic, bay leaves, and golden turmeric.

🥗 Dohkhlieh (Pork Herb Salad) Zesty Salad

Tender boiled pork tossed with freshly sliced red onions, finely diced ginger, fiery green chilies, and garnished with soft boiled egg slices.

🥟 Pumaloi (Steamed Rice Cakes) Traditional Bread

Fluffy powdered rice steamed inside unique earthen pots called 'Khiew Ranei' over boiling water, served warm with spicy curries or hot tea.

🫘 Tungrymbai (Fermented Soybean) Earthy Relish

Fermented indigenous soybeans slow-cooked and sautéed with thick roasted black sesame paste, garlic, ginger, and small chunks of pork.

🥞 Pukhlein (Crispy Jaggery Fritter) Tea-Time Sweet

Crispy golden-brown sweet flatbread prepared from a batter of freshly ground rice powder and pure organic cane jaggery, deep-fried to perfection.

🥩 Dohneiiong (Black Sesame Curry) Rich Gravy

Tender pork cuts simmered in a dark, earthy, nutty gravy made from roasted black sesame seeds (Neiiong), green chilies, and Khasi spices.

✨ Foodie Tip: Pair warm steaming Jadoh with Dohneiiong and fiery Tungrymbai at traditional Khasi food stalls in Shillong's Police Bazar!

Culture & Festivities

Sacred Festivals & Cultural Calendar

🎉

Experience ancient matrilineal dances, the Hundred Drums harvest, and sacred healing rites:

🌸 Shad Suk Mynsiem (Dance of Peaceful Hearts)
April (Springtime)

The grandest annual Khasi thanksgiving dance festival held at Weiking Ground in Shillong, where maidens in gold and silk and men with silver quivers dance in graceful concentric circles to the beats of the Tangmuri and Ksing drum.

👑 Nongkrem Dance Festival (Smit)
November (Autumn)

A 5-day religious ceremony at the royal palace in Smit honoring Goddess Ka Blei Synshar for peace and bumper harvests, presided over by the Syiem of Khyrim and the High Priestess (Ka Syiem Sad).

🥁 Wangala Festival (100 Drums Festival)
November (Post-Harvest)

The premier post-harvest festival of the Garo tribe honoring the Sun God Saljong, marked by 100 drummers beating long cylindrical wooden drums in thunderous unison while dancers in feathered headdresses move in rhythm.

🌊 Behdienkhlam Festival (Jowai)
July (Monsoon Sowing)

The most important socio-religious festival of the Jaintia people in Jowai, dedicated to driving away disease and plague ('Khlam') through symbolic ritual tree trunks ('Deinkhlas') and football matches in the sacred mud pool of Aitnar.

✨ Cultural Tip: Experience the grand 100-drum synchronized rhythm during the Wangala Festival in the Garo Hills!
